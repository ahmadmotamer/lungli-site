Portuguese–English language exchange: find a partner and practise both

By the Lungli team · Published 17 November 2026 · 4 min read

Brazil has one of the largest communities of English learners in the world, and more English speakers learn Portuguese every year for work, travel and music. That makes Portuguese and English a pair where partners are easy to find. This guide shows how, which Portuguese to choose, and the mistakes each of you will help the other fix.

Who you can meet

  • If you speak English and learn Portuguese, most of your partners will be in Brazil; others are in Portugal, Angola and Mozambique.
  • If you speak Portuguese and learn English, look for partners in the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia who are learning Portuguese.

Time zones: São Paulo is two to three hours ahead of New York, and Lisbon shares the clock with London.

Which Portuguese? Brazil or Portugal

The two understand each other, but the accent and some everyday words differ:

BrazilPortugal
"You" in everyday speechvocêtu
A busônibusautocarro
A mobile phonecelulartelemóvel
Breakfastcafé da manhãpequeno-almoço

Choose the Portuguese of the country you are interested in. If you have no reason to prefer one, Brazilian Portuguese is the one you will hear most online. Say which you want in your profile.

Mistakes Portuguese speakers often make in English

  • "I have 30 years" instead of "I am 30" — in Portuguese you *have* years (*tenho 30 anos*).
  • "Is raining" instead of "It is raining" — English always needs a subject.
  • "Push" and "pull". *Puxar* means pull, so doors marked *push* are a classic trap.
  • "Pretend" for *pretender*. *Pretender* is "to intend"; *pretend* means to act as if something were true.
  • An extra vowel at the end: *Facebooki*, *nighti*. Practise ending words on the consonant.

Mistakes English speakers often make in Portuguese

  • Nasal vowels. *Pão* (bread), *mãe* (mother) and *não* (no) need the sound through the nose; ask your partner to repeat them slowly.
  • Ser and estar. Both mean "to be". *Ser* is for what something is; *estar* for how or where it is now.
  • "Esquisito" for "exquisite". It means "strange".
  • "Puxar" for "push", the same trap in the other direction.
  • Gender and agreement: *o carro novo*, *a casa nova*.

A first message that gets an answer

Say who you are, what you offer and ask one real question. Writing it in the language your partner speaks shows effort:

  • In Portuguese, from an English speaker: "Oi! Meu nome é Tom e sou de Leeds. Estou aprendendo português e vi que você está aprendendo inglês. Quer praticar comigo? Posso ajudar você com o inglês."
  • In English, from a Portuguese speaker: "Hi, I am Ana from Recife. I am learning English and I see you are learning Portuguese. Would you like to practise together? I can help you with your Portuguese."

A 30-minute session for this pair

MinutesWhat you do
0–5Greetings and news in Portuguese
5–15One topic in Portuguese; the Portuguese speaker corrects the two or three mistakes that matter most
15–25The same topic in English; the English speaker corrects
25–30Each of you writes down the words and corrections to review

Topics that work well for Portuguese and English

  • Music: bossa nova, samba, funk, fado — share a song and talk about its words.
  • Football, the easiest way to start a conversation with almost any Brazilian.
  • Food: feijoada, pão de queijo, pastel de nata — and their English equivalents, if any exist.
  • Expressions: *saudade*, *jeitinho*, *cafuné* — words English needs a sentence for.

Lungli is a free language exchange that works in your browser, in Portuguese as well as English.
