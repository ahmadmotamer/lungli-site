How to stay safe in an online language exchange

Most language exchange partners are exactly what they seem: people who want to practise a language. A few simple habits keep it that way for you, and make it easy to walk away when something feels wrong.

Keep personal details personal

You can have a great exchange without anyone knowing your surname, your address, your workplace or your phone number. Share them only with people you have come to trust, and never because someone pushes for them.

Be careful with photos as well: a picture of your street, your school uniform or your office badge can say more than you meant to.

Stay inside the app at first

An app made for language exchange gives you tools a private messenger does not: blocking, reporting, and control over who can write to you. Stay there until you know someone well. A partner who insists on moving to another app straight away, for no clear reason, is a warning sign.

Know the warning signs

Most problems announce themselves early. Be careful when someone:

  • Steers away from the language towards flirting or compliments about your looks.
  • Asks for photos, especially private ones.
  • Asks for money, gift cards, help with a payment or an "investment".
  • Pressures you to reply faster, share more, or keep the conversation secret.
  • Tells a story that keeps changing, or avoids every simple question about themselves.

You do not owe anyone an explanation. If a conversation makes you uncomfortable, you can stop.

Block and report

Blocking stops someone from contacting you. Reporting tells the people who run the app, so they can stop that person from doing the same to others. Use both. A report is not an accusation you have to prove; it is information that helps keep everyone safe.

Meeting in person

If you ever decide to meet a partner face to face, talk on the app first for a while, meet somewhere public, tell a friend where you are going, and get there and back on your own.

Teenagers

If you are under 18, use an app that keeps teenagers separate from adults, and tell an adult you trust if anyone makes you uncomfortable.

How Lungli keeps you safe

Lungli is for practising languages, not dating: flirting with people who have not asked for it, sexual messages and asking for photos break the rules.
