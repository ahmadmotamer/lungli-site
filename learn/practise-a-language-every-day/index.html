How to practise a language every day, even when you are busy

Daily practice beats long weekend sessions — but only if it fits your day. Here is how to build a language habit that survives work, study and tired evenings.

Why every day matters

Memory fades fast. A word you learn on Monday is half gone by Thursday unless you meet it again. Short, daily contact with a language keeps what you learned alive and makes each new session start where the last one ended, not from zero.

The good news: "every day" does not mean "an hour a day". Ten minutes is enough, if those minutes are well used.

Attach it to something you already do

Habits stick when they have a trigger. Pick something you do every day anyway and add your language to it:

  • With your morning coffee: reply to one message in the language you are learning.
  • On the bus or train: read through the corrections and words you saved yesterday.
  • During lunch: write three sentences about your morning.
  • Before bed: send your partner a short message about your day.

Make it about people, not apps

Flashcards and exercises are useful, but they are easy to skip. A person waiting for your reply is not. A language exchange partner turns practice into a conversation you want to continue — and a conversation is the best practice there is.

Agree on a rhythm with your partner: one message a day each, in each other's language. It takes five minutes, and after a month you will have written thirty small texts and read thirty more.

Use the small gaps

A busy day still has gaps: waiting for a meeting, standing in a queue, the five minutes before dinner. Keep your practice small enough to fit into them. A single written message, three new words, or one correction to read again all count.

Review before you learn more

Before you learn anything new, spend two minutes on what you already met: yesterday's corrections, last week's new words. Reviewing is what turns "I saw this once" into "I know this". It is also the fastest way to stop making the same mistake.

Track your streak — gently

Write down each day you practised. Seeing a row of days is motivating; seeing a gap is a reminder. But if you miss a day, simply start again. A habit is what you do most days, not something you lose after one bad one.
