12 tips for your first language exchange

Your first language exchange can feel strange: you are about to make mistakes in front of a stranger, on purpose. These tips make it easier — and make every conversation count.

Before you start

  • Set a simple goal. "Talk about my weekend in Spanish" is a better goal than "become fluent". Small goals are easy to reach and easy to measure.
  • Prepare a few sentences. Introduce yourself, say why you are learning, and prepare two or three questions. You will feel calmer with them ready.
  • Agree how you will split the time. Half in each language is the usual way. Say it at the start, so nobody ends up speaking only their own language.

During the conversation

  • Keep it simple. Short sentences you are sure of are better than long ones that collapse halfway through.
  • Ask when you do not understand. "Can you say that more simply?" is one of the most useful sentences you can learn.
  • Talk about real things. Your city, your food, your work, a film you liked. You remember words better when they matter to you.
  • Note new words. Write down the words and phrases you meet, with the sentence they came in.

Correcting each other

  • Agree how much you want to be corrected. Some people want every mistake; most prefer only the important ones.
  • Correct kindly. Show the natural way to say it, and add a short note on why if you can. Avoid long lectures.
  • Thank your partner for corrections. It encourages them to keep helping.

After the conversation

  • Review what you saved. Look at your corrections and new words the next day, and again a few days later. That is when they stick.
  • Come back. One conversation helps a little; one a week for three months changes everything. A regular partner is worth more than many one-off chats.
