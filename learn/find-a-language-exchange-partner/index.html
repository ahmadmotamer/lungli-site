How to find a language exchange partner online

The hardest part of a language exchange is often the first step: finding someone who speaks the language you are learning, is learning yours, and actually replies. Here is how to find a good partner — and how to keep one.

Look for a two-way match

The best partner is a two-way match: they speak the language you are learning, and they are learning a language you speak. That way you both give and you both get. If only one of you is learning, the exchange tends to fade.

Some apps match people by language for you. On Lungli, the people you see first are two-way matches, and each one comes with a reason you match — shared goals, overlapping free time, a time zone close to yours.

Check what they want to practise

Two people learning the same language can want very different things. One wants to chat casually; another wants help with grammar for an exam; a third wants slang. Before you write, look at what your possible partner wants to improve and how they like to practise — text, voice, or meeting up. A shared goal makes for a much better exchange.

Mind the time zone

If you want to chat in real time, a partner in a nearby time zone is easier. If you prefer slow, thoughtful messages, the time zone matters less — someone on the other side of the world can be a wonderful letter-writing partner.

Write a good first message

Many first messages go unanswered because they are too short ("hi") or too long. A good first message:

  • says who you are in one or two sentences;
  • says what you would like to practise and why you wrote to them;
  • asks a question that is easy to answer;
  • is written partly in their language, even if it is simple — it shows you are serious.

For example: "Hi! I'm Sara from Cairo. I'm learning Spanish (around A2) and saw you're learning Arabic. Would you like to practise together? ¿Cuál es tu ciudad favorita?"

Keep it going

  • Reply within a day or two, or say when you will.
  • Share the time fairly between both languages.
  • Correct gently and only sometimes. Agree together how much correcting you each want.
  • Save what you learn, so the same mistake does not come back next week.

Stay safe

Most people in language exchange communities are there to learn, but take the usual care:

  • keep the conversation inside the app until you trust the person;
  • never send money to someone you met online, whatever the reason;
  • do not share your address, phone number or other private details early on;
  • block and report anyone who makes you uncomfortable.
