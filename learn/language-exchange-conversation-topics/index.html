40 conversation topics for a language exchange

The first minutes of a language exchange are the hardest: you have said hello, you have said where you are from — and now what? Having a few topics ready takes the pressure off. Here are forty, grouped from easy to deeper, that work in any language and at almost any level.

Easy topics for beginners

  • Where you live, and what you can see from your window.
  • Your family, or the people you live with.
  • What you ate today.
  • Your favourite food from your country, and how it is made.
  • What a normal weekday looks like for you.
  • The weather where you are right now.
  • Your favourite colour, season or time of day — and why.
  • Pets you have, or would like to have.
  • What you did last weekend.
  • What you will do this weekend.

Topics for intermediate learners

  • Why you are learning this language.
  • A place in your country a visitor should not miss.
  • A film, series or book you liked recently.
  • Music you listen to, and a song you would recommend.
  • Your job or studies, and what you like about them.
  • Holidays and celebrations in your country.
  • Something that surprised you about your partner's country.
  • Sport: what people play and watch where you live.
  • A city you would love to live in.
  • A skill you would like to learn.

Topics that go deeper

  • Something your country does better than others — and something it does worse.
  • How life in your city has changed in the last ten years.
  • Habits that are polite in your culture and rude in others.
  • What makes a good friend.
  • Technology: what helps you, and what you would happily give up.
  • A decision that changed your life.
  • How people in your country spend their money.
  • The best advice you were ever given.
  • What you would change about the way languages are taught.
  • Where you see yourself in five years.

Questions that help you learn the language itself

  • "How would you say this more naturally?"
  • "Is this word formal or casual?"
  • "What do people really say instead of this?"
  • "Can you explain this joke or expression?"
  • "Which mistakes do learners of your language make most?"
  • "How do young people say this?"
  • "Is there a word in your language that has no translation?"
  • "Can you correct my last message?"
  • "What should I read or watch to learn more?"
  • "What do you find hardest in my language?"

How to use these topics

Pick two or three before you start. Ask, listen, and ask a follow-up question — "why?", "what was that like?", "and in your country?" — before moving on. A good topic is not one you finish quickly; it is one that leads somewhere.
