What to look for in a language exchange app

There are many ways to find a language exchange partner: local meetups, forums, social media groups and apps made for it. If you choose an app, a few things decide whether you will actually learn — or just collect unanswered messages. Here is what to look for.

Matching that works both ways

A language exchange only works when both people get something. Look for an app that matches you with people who speak the language you are learning and are learning yours — not just anyone who lists the language. Good apps also tell you why someone is a match: shared goals, overlapping free time, a time zone close to yours.

A way to search beyond the suggestions

Suggestions are a good start, but sometimes you want something specific: a speaker of a particular language, or a learner at a particular level. Check that you can search by the language someone speaks and the one they are learning.

Corrections built into the chat

Correcting each other is the point of an exchange. In a plain messenger, corrections get lost between other messages. An app made for language exchange lets your partner correct a message right where it is, with the natural version and a short explanation.

A place to keep what you learn

You will meet new words and get corrections every day. If they disappear into the chat history, you will make the same mistakes again. Look for a way to save corrections and words and review them later.

Groups for when one-to-one is too much

Sometimes it is easier to start in a group: lower pressure, more people, more chance someone replies. Groups by language and level are a good sign.

Safety and privacy

Language exchange apps put strangers in contact, so check:

  • Can you block and report people, and are reports private?
  • Can you control who writes to you — for example, only people you have already talked to?
  • Is your email address kept private, with messages going through the app?
  • Are teenagers protected — separated from adults?
  • Is it clearly not a dating app, with rules against flirting and requests for photos?

The price — and the ads

Many apps are free with limits, ads, or a paid plan for the features that matter. Check what you get for free: the number of messages, the corrections, the search. An exchange should not cost money — you are both giving your language.

Where you can use it

Some apps work only on a phone. If you like to write longer messages, an app that also works in a computer's browser is a real advantage.
