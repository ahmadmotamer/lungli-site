Russian–English language exchange: find a partner and practise both

By the Lungli team · Published 17 November 2026 · 5 min read

Russian looks hard from the outside — a new alphabet, six cases — and English looks easy until you meet its articles and tenses. A partner on the other side makes both less frightening. This guide shows how to find one, how to read Cyrillic quickly, and the mistakes each of you will help the other fix.

Who you can meet

  • If you speak English and learn Russian, your partners are in Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, and in Russian-speaking communities across Europe, Israel and Central Asia.
  • If you speak Russian and learn English, look for partners in the United Kingdom, the United States, Canada and Australia who are learning Russian for study, work or literature.

Time zones: Moscow is three hours ahead of London in winter; Russia itself spans eleven, so ask your partner where they are.

Reading Cyrillic: the letters that trick you

Many Cyrillic letters look like Latin ones but stand for other sounds. Learn these first, and most words become readable:

LetterSounds likeExample
В вvвода (voda), water
Н нnнет (net), no
Р рrрука (ruka), hand
С сsсок (sok), juice
У уooутро (utro), morning
Х хkh, as in Scottish "loch"хлеб (khleb), bread

Mistakes Russian speakers often make in English

  • Missing "a" and "the" — Russian has no articles: "I saw film" instead of "I saw a film".
  • "I very like it" instead of "I really like it" or "I like it very much" — *очень* does not go before a verb in English.
  • "Magazine" for *магазин*. *Магазин* is a shop; a magazine is *журнал*.
  • "Sympathetic" for *симпатичный*. *Симпатичный* means "nice-looking"; *sympathetic* means "showing understanding".
  • "I never was in London" instead of "I have never been to London" — English uses the present perfect for what you have or have not done in your life.

Mistakes English speakers often make in Russian

  • Cases. The end of a noun changes with its role: *книга* (a book), *у меня нет книги* (I do not have a book), *я читаю книгу* (I am reading a book).
  • Ты and вы. *Ты* is for friends, family and children; *вы* for anyone else, and for more than one person. Start with *вы*.
  • Aspect. Most verbs come in pairs: *делать* (to be doing) and *сделать* (to get done). Choosing the right one is harder than the tense.
  • Stress. It can fall on any syllable and is not written, and it changes the sound of the vowels. Ask your partner to mark it on new words.
  • The sound ы, which English does not have. Try saying "i" with your tongue pulled back.

A first message that gets an answer

Say who you are, what you offer and ask one real question. Writing it in the language your partner speaks shows effort:

  • In Russian, from an English speaker: "Здравствуйте! Меня зовут Том, я из Лидса. Я изучаю русский язык и вижу, что вы изучаете английский. Хотите практиковаться вместе со мной? Я могу помочь вам с английским."
  • In English, from a Russian speaker: "Hello, I am Olga from Kazan. I am learning English and I see you are learning Russian. Would you like to practise together? I can help you with your Russian."

A 30-minute session for this pair

MinutesWhat you do
0–5Greetings and news in Russian
5–15One topic in Russian; the Russian speaker corrects the two or three mistakes that matter most
15–25The same topic in English; the English speaker corrects
25–30Each of you writes down the words and corrections to review

Topics that work well for Russian and English

  • Books and films: a Russian classic and an English one, each explained by the other's reader.
  • Winter: how people dress, travel and celebrate when it is very cold.
  • Food: blini, borscht, pelmeni — and a recipe from your side.
  • Sayings: *без труда не вытащишь и рыбку из пруда* and its English cousins.

Practise Russian and English on Lungli

Lungli is a free language exchange that works in your browser, in Russian as well as English.
